Three problems with this metric

Problem 1
Wrong question
Their claim: Top-10 Salaries as % of total wages declining = no disproportionate growth
This metric measures individual pay concentration, not tier growth. Adding 7 new $150K leadership roles makes this % go down because the denominator grows faster than the numerator. That is exactly what happened.
Problem 2
Mismatched denominator
Their FY2026 denominator: $16,738,447 (labeled "Total Regular Wages Budgeted")
"Total Regular Wages Budgeted" is an internal budget figure, not a number you can pull from Transparent Utah. The actual FY2026 salary total in Transparent Utah is $15,969,903 — a gap of $768,544. They cite Transparent Utah as their source on slide 2 but their denominator does not come from it.
Problem 3
FY2026 data used without acknowledgment
Slide 4: "FY26 data isn't in yet"
Slide 10 uses FY2026 figures. The FY2026 data was live on Transparent Utah around the same time as this presentation. If the data was too preliminary to cite as a source, it was too preliminary to use as evidence. Full roster below.

Their budget growth metric

"6% Net Expenditure Increase"

— SLCPL Presentation to Board, July 27, 2026, Slide 3. FY2019 ($22.3M) to FY2025 ($36.3M).

The actual total growth is +62.8%. Here is how they arrived at 6%, and what it omits.

Their formula (reverse-engineered): FY2025 actuals: $36.3M minus pass-throughs: − $5.0M Adjusted FY2025: $31.3M ($31.3M ÷ $22.3M) ^ (1 ÷ 6) − 1 = 5.8% → "6%" This formula is called CAGR: Compound Annual Growth Rate. It answers: if growth had been perfectly smooth, what % would each year add? The (1 ÷ 6) exponent converts 6 years of total growth into a per-year rate. CAGR is a legitimate measure. It is also the smallest defensible number you can attach to a 62.8% total increase. The pass-through adjustment is disclosed on slide 2.

Why net-to-net is still misleading: stripping pass-throughs from FY2025 but not from FY2019 makes the starting point look smaller than the end point relative to the same basis. Applied consistently to both years, total growth is still +40.4% and CAGR is still 5.8%. Even their own adjustment, done fairly, produces the same rate. The issue is presenting that rate without the +40% total or any inflation context.

Unadjusted (same basis, both years)+62.8%+8.5%/yr($36.3M − $22.3M) ÷ $22.3M

The pass-through adjustment is legitimate. Transparent Utah does double-count capital and debt service. Applied consistently to both years, total growth is still +40%. The 6% figure survives a fair adjustment. The +62.8% total and the inflation gap do not appear anywhere in their presentation.

What the presentation did not address

The compensation study

The CEO's FY27 budget letter cites a "2025 third-party compensation study" as the basis for the restructuring and 5.5% COLA. It has never been published, presented in a public board session, or posted to the library's website. Mr. Baskett was hired as Executive Director; his title later changed to CEO and Library Director. The study was conducted after that title change. No one outside the organization can evaluate its assumptions.

The employee satisfaction survey

Multiple current employees report the survey is no longer anonymous and that questions have changed in ways that shape results. The ask: run it anonymously, consistent questions, publish the results.
