The soft coercion framingThe email states simultaneously: "there are no near-term plans regarding any layoffs" AND the program exists to "minimize the potential need for involuntary layoffs or separations in the future." These statements together communicate: take the buyout now, or face the possibility of forced cuts later.
Who sent it mattersLana Hernandez, People & Culture, administered this program. The People & Culture department replaced HR in the FY27 restructuring — it is one of the new roles created by the same leadership expansion the VSIP appears designed to offset.
Three budget signals that predicted the VSIP — all present before the announcement
Signal 1: Flex wages tripled FY26→FY27 (+208%)FY27 Budget Doc p.10 (Salaries & Wages — Flex)
Flex wages = part-time and temporary workers. The library budgeted $519K in FY27 Flex wages vs $168K in FY26 — a 208% increase. Full-time benefited positions exit via VSIP; they are replaced with part-time flex workers at lower total cost. Total FTE stays flat at 249.675 on paper.
Signal 2: Employee insurance decreases despite 10% premium increaseFY27 Budget Doc p.10 (Employee Insurance line)
The budget doc (p.10) explicitly states a projected 10% premium increase. Yet the Employee Insurance budget line decreases from $2,617,144 (FY26) to $2,606,857 (FY27). Insurance costs can only fall while premiums rise if the number of insured employees falls.
Signal 3: Vacancy savings growing every year FY22–FY25Eide Bailly audits FY22–FY25 — budgetary highlights section each year
Every year for 4 consecutive years, personnel came in significantly under budget due to vacant positions. FY23 is the outlier — the only year personnel ran over its final adopted budget, due to management turnover and 18 new FTE. FY26 Q3 pace suggests ~$1.5M projected under budget again.
